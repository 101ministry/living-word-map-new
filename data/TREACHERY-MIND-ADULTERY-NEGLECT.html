Question: What mind must the adulterer hold against God to justify these acts? How do Neglect and Abandonment differ? How does this connect to dishonoring parents when children rob or curse — even after Biblical upbringing?

I. The Mind of the Adulterer — Against God, Able to Justify Everything Else

In the Topics material, adultery appears twice — and both readings matter:

EntryRootsFruit / Principality
#188 (Whoredom)addiction/bondage + control/rebellionSexual Corruption → Whoredom
#251 (Treachery cluster)addiction/bondageDivision and Relational Destruction → Treachery Against Others

Adultery is not merely “a slip.” It is a mind that has already relocated lordship: from covenant under God to appetite + autonomy.

That mind, to remain against God and continue sinning, must become a self-justifying treachery-mind. It typically holds these convictions (often unspoken):

  1. “My covenant is negotiable.” Marriage is treated as conditional on feeling, gain, or control — not as bound before God. Once that is accepted, neglect, abandonment, robbery, and cursing are no longer unthinkable; they are downstream.
  2. “I am entitled to take what I want.” Adultery is theft of body, trust, and honor. The same entitlement fuels robbery (covetousness/materialism) and cursing (bitterness when blocked).
  3. “Presence without faithfulness counts as love.” That is the bridge to neglect: remain in the role, exit the duty.
  4. “When cost rises, I may exit entirely.” That is abandonment: not failure to feed — failure to remain in the bond God joined.
  5. “God’s law applies to others, not to my exception.” Control/rebellion root: I am above the terms I still preach.
  6. “Words don’t bind me; only my appetite does.” Vows mean nothing; speech becomes weapon or cover.

Under Principality of Treachery Against Others, adultery sits in one row with neglect, abandonment, robbery, cursing, and dishonoring. Same parent. Same fruit: Division and Relational Destruction.

The adulterer’s mind is not merely “weak.” It is treacherous: trained to break covenants while narrating innocence — and once treachery is seated, every other act in that list becomes morally available.

The chart also lists abandoning of the family by the father or mother under Rebellion (deception/falsehood root) — abandonment as active rebellion, not passive drift. Neglect can look respectable; abandonment often exposes the treachery neglect concealed.

III. Dishonoring Parents When Children Rob or Curse

Dishonoring (#258) carries root pride and self-exaltation — same Treachery cluster, immediately after cursing in the list.

A. Faithful parents, treacherous children — still possible

Biblical upbringing does not mechanically prevent robbery or cursing. Scripture gives clear cases:

  • Eli instructed Israel; his sons were corrupt (1 Sam 2:22–25).
  • Samuel was faithful; his sons took bribes and perverted justice (1 Sam 8:3).

The child’s robbery (covetousness) and cursing (bitterness) are their treachery — not automatic proof the parents failed to teach. Each soul chooses its lord.

A child can dishonor parents while parents honored God. That is the child’s pride, not necessarily the parents’ neglect.

B. Adultery → neglect → abandonment opens the same door in the house

When the adulterer’s mind runs the home, children inherit more than doctrine:

Parent’s treacheryWhat the child learns (atmosphere, not lesson)
AdulteryCovenants are breakable; bodies and trust are commodities
NeglectGod-talk without faithful care; authority without stewardship
AbandonmentPeople leave when cost appears; love has an exit
Robbery (if modeled or tolerated)Wanting = taking
Cursing (if modeled)Speech destroys; bitterness is power

Even with Bible reading at table, the living covenant may teach: words bind others, not me. Treachery passes not always by explicit teaching, but by broken relational order.

C. Where “dishonoring parents” connects

Exodus 20:12 / Deuteronomy 5:16 — honor father and mother.
Deuteronomy 27:16 — cursed is he who dishonors father or mother.

Dishonoring is not only insult. It includes:

  • treating parents’ teaching as worthless while demanding their resources;
  • shaming their name through robbery or cursing;
  • reversing roles — child judges, mocks, steals from, or curses those God set above them.

When a child robs or curses others, they often dishonor parents in three ways at once:

  1. Shame on the house — the family name bears the fruit (Prov 10:1; 17:25).
  2. Nullification of stewardship — “you raised me in vain” weaponized against godly parents.
  3. Pride — “I am my own law” (#258 root) — dishonor, because honor recognizes God’s order, not self-rule.

D. Biblical upbringing, treacherous children

Two truths hold together:

1. The child is accountable. Robbery and cursing are not “parent fault only.” They are treachery chosen under covetousness and bitterness — often fed by pride.

2. The whole house can still sit under Treachery’s fruit. Faithful teaching in a home where adultery, neglect, or abandonment occurred (now or in the line) means the child received two voices: Scripture at the table, treachery in the walls.

Neglect can be subtle in “Biblical” homes — truth taught, soul not attended; discipline without tenderness; presence without repentance after sin. That produces the loneliness/emotional brokenness root shared by neglect and abandonment. Lonely, broken children often become robbers (take to fill lack) or cursers (speak to wound back).

IV. One Chain — How the Topics Connect

ADULTERY (covenant broken; appetite + rebellion seated) ↓ NEGLECT (still present, but faithless — chair occupied, duty refused) ↓ ABANDONMENT (presence or covenant withdrawn) ↓ Child inherits: broken trust, loneliness, division ↓ ROBBERY (take by force/stealth — covetousness fills the gap) ↓ CURSING (bitterness speaks — destroys with words) ↓ DISHONORING (pride — child exalts self above parents and God’s order)

All under Principality of Treachery Against Others — Division and Relational Destruction.

V. Summary

The adulterer’s mind is a treacherous, self-sovereign mind: it breaks covenant, then rationalizes every further breach — neglect, abandonment, theft, and cursing — because God’s law has already been demoted below appetite and control.

Neglect is faithless presence; abandonment is withdrawn presence. Both fracture the same bond from different angles.

Dishonoring parents when children rob or curse is the child’s pride expressing the same treachery cluster — but it lands on parents with special force because God joined honor to stewardship. Biblical upbringing can be real and still coexist with a child’s treachery; yet adultery and neglect in the line often train the child in the very robbery and cursing that dishonors the parents who taught otherwise.
