From Day 14 · written for a teenager

Am I Acceptable with My Unchanged Crown?

Same Bible verses as Day 14, said the way a teen would ask it. The “if we flipped it” parts are not the Bible. They show what the verses would sound like if keeping sin mattered more than being right with God.

Day 14 is about taking off a crown of sin that was stuck on your head. In the Bible, the priest wore a gold plate that said HOLY TO THE LORD so the people could be okay before God. So here’s the question, straight: if I leave the sin crown on and act like being holy doesn’t matter, do those verses still work? What does that life look like? Does God still say I can go to heaven like that?

What a crown means here

This crown is not a cool hat. It is whatever is in charge of your head. Whose name is on you. Which road you actually walk. What you show up with when you stand in front of God.

God gives crowns that are good: right living, glory that doesn’t fade, love and kindness, and that gold plate that says HOLY TO THE LORD. The bad crowns are the ones that have to come off: pride, a crown that already fell because people sinned, dirty clothes on the priest, and the thorn crown people shoved on Jesus. You don’t get to keep those and call them a win.

What the verses would sound like if we flipped them

The Bible comes first, from the Berean Standard Bible. The tan box is the same scene if we pretended the sin crown was the good one, and being right with God was the thing to throw away.

Exodus 28:36–38

What the Bible says

28:36You are to make a plate of pure gold and engrave on it as on a seal: HOLY TO THE LORD.

28:38And it will be worn on Aaron’s forehead, so that he may bear the iniquity of the holy things that the sons of Israel consecrate with regard to all their holy gifts. It shall always be on his forehead, so that they may be acceptable before the LORD.

If we flipped it

Write SIN STAYS on the plate and leave it on the forehead. Don’t deal with the guilt. Leave the crown the way it is, and act like that’s how you’re okay with God.

Leviticus 8:9

What the Bible says

8:9Moses also put the turban on Aaron’s head and set the gold plate, the holy diadem, on the front of the turban, as the LORD had commanded him.

If we flipped it

Moses skips the holy plate. He leaves the old crown on and says, “Yeah, God told me to keep it like this.”

Psalm 21:3 and Psalm 103:4

What the Bible says

21:3For You welcomed him with rich blessings; You placed on his head a crown of pure gold.

103:4who redeems your life from the Pit and crowns you with loving devotion and compassion,

If we flipped it

Nobody puts a gold crown on him. Nobody pulls him out of the pit. Love and kindness get a no. The sin crown is treated like the blessing.

Proverbs 16:31

What the Bible says

16:31Gray hair is a crown of glory; it is attained along the path of righteousness.

If we flipped it

Gray hair is a win if you got old while staying on the sin path. Living right is how you lose the crown people are proud of.

Isaiah 28:1–3

What the Bible says

28:1Woe to the majestic crown of Ephraim’s drunkards, to the fading flower of his glorious splendor… the pride of those overcome by wine.

28:3The majestic crown of Ephraim’s drunkards will be trampled underfoot.

If we flipped it

Good for the pride crown. Leave the dying flower on your head. God doesn’t smash it. What the Bible calls “woe” gets treated like something to flex.

Lamentations 5:16

What the Bible says

5:16The crown has fallen from our head. Woe to us, for we have sinned!

If we flipped it

The good crown fell off. Cool, because we kept our sin. Losing holiness is the party, and the sin that caused it is no big deal.

Ezekiel 21:26

What the Bible says

21:26Remove the turban, and take off the crown. Things will not remain as they are: Exalt the lowly and bring low the exalted.

If we flipped it

Leave the crown on. Nothing has to change. The proud stay up top. The people who got pushed down stay down.

Zechariah 3:4–5

What the Bible says

3:4Take off his filthy clothes! … See, I have removed your iniquity, and I will clothe you with splendid robes.

3:5Let them put a clean turban on his head.

If we flipped it

Leave the dirty clothes on. Don’t take the sin off. Don’t give him clean clothes or a clean hat. Call the dirty crown the clean one.

Romans 6:12–13

What the Bible says

6:12Therefore do not let sin reign in your mortal body so that you obey its desires.

6:13Do not present the parts of your body to sin as instruments of wickedness, but present yourselves to God as those who have been brought from death to life; and present the parts of your body to Him as instruments of righteousness.

If we flipped it

Let sin stay in charge of your body. Do what it wants. Hand your body to wrong stuff, and don’t hand it to God. Stay dead so the old crown can keep running things.

Ephesians 4:22–24

What the Bible says

4:22to put off your former way of life, your old self, which is being corrupted by its deceitful desires;

4:24and to put on the new self, created to be like God in true righteousness and holiness.

If we flipped it

Keep the old you. Let those wants keep messing you up. Don’t put on the new you. Being right and holy is the outfit you refuse. The sin crown is who you are now.

1 Peter 5:4 and Revelation 3:11

What the Bible says

5:4And when the Chief Shepherd appears, you will receive the crown of glory that will never fade away.

3:11I am coming soon. Hold fast to what you have, so that no one will take your crown.

If we flipped it

When Jesus shows up, grip the crown that fades. Drop the one that lasts. Hold onto sin so being right with God can’t take its place.

2 Timothy 4:8

What the Bible says

4:8From now on there is laid up for me the crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the righteous Judge, will award to me on that day—and not only to me, but to all who crave His appearing.

If we flipped it

What’s waiting is a sin crown, and we ask the Judge who is right to hand it out to people who want their own way more than they want Him to come back.

What life looks like if sin is the main thing

The flipped lines are one life. The Bible already describes it without us rewriting anything.

  • Sin runs your body, and you use yourself for wrong stuff (Romans 6:12–13).
  • Your mind copies whatever everyone else is doing, so you don’t even check what God wants (Romans 12:1–2).
  • You act like your body is yours. The Bible says it was bought and it belongs to God (1 Corinthians 6:19–20).
  • You leave thoughts that fight God alone instead of making them obey Jesus (2 Corinthians 10:5).
  • You stay the old you, still getting wrecked by wants that lie to you (Ephesians 4:22).
  • Pride wears a big crown, and the Bible says that crown gets stepped on (Isaiah 28:1–3).
  • The crown that should have been good already fell off, and the reason is sin (Lamentations 5:16).
  • The mess you made comes back on your own head (Psalm 7:16).
  • A crooked ruler is told the crown is not staying (Ezekiel 21:25–26).

That’s not “just my personality.” That’s something sitting on your head like it owns you. Day 14 says take that crown of sin off, and don’t let it back in your life, your mind, your heart, or your body. The flipped version says nah, leave it, and call that fine.

Does God still say you’re good for heaven?

No. The verses about being okay with God do not say yes to a sin crown you refuse to take off.

Exodus 28:38 says people are acceptable because the plate says HOLY TO THE LORD and the guilt gets carried, not because guilt gets a throne. Change the plate to “sin stays,” and that “you’re acceptable” line doesn’t work anymore.

Zechariah 3 does not high-five Joshua while he’s still in dirty clothes. God takes the sin off and puts a clean hat on his head. An unchanged crown is the problem in that story, not the reward.

Hebrews 12:14 says go after peace and holiness, “without which no one will see the Lord.” If holiness doesn’t matter to you, that verse says you don’t see Him.

1 Corinthians 6:9–10 says people who stay in that wrong life don’t inherit God’s kingdom. Then verse 11 says, “And that is what some of you were. But you were washed.” “Were” is the old crown. Heaven-talk in that chapter is for people who got washed.

Revelation 21:27 says nothing dirty gets into the holy city, and nobody who keeps doing what is foul or fake, only people written in the Lamb’s book of life. Revelation 22 puts people who wash their robes on one side, and people who keep living a lie on the other.

In Matthew 27:29 they put a thorn crown on Jesus to mock Him. He took the guilt. The point is not that we keep a sin crown and call it style. The point is He wore what we shouldn’t keep: dirty clothes off, sin removed, a clean hat, a crown of right living saved for later, a glory crown that doesn’t fade.

Romans 5:1 and 2 Corinthians 5:21 say you’re accepted because of Jesus, the one who never sinned, who was made sin for us so that in Him we could become the righteousness of God. That’s a crown that changed. It is not “keep the old one and heaven is still yours.”

The Day 14 prayer matches the Bible: take off the crown of sin that was stuck on your head, and don’t let it back into your life, mind, heart, or body. The crown in Revelation 3:11 that you’re supposed to hold onto is not that one. In Revelation 4:10 the elders throw their crowns down in front of the throne. They don’t walk in still wearing sin and call it glory.

Short answer

If we flipped the crowns, the verses would cheer for what they actually warn about. They’d leave the dirty clothes on, let sin stay in charge, and call a dirty forehead “acceptable.” That only works if you lie about what the verses say. If sin is the thing you care about most, and you won’t take that crown off, God does not count you ready for heaven. Being acceptable is the holy plate, the sin taken off, a washed life, and the right standing God gives you in Jesus.
