Fruits of Identities

How listening to R&R Day 48 familiar identities counteracts the life Scripture commands
Philippians 4:8–9, 12–13 · Galatians 5 · 1 Corinthians 13

Question: How does listening to the topics of Day 48 counteract or denounce what the Bible says we ought to live?

Day 48 (exact titles): Familiar Identity Of Envy; Escapism; Fantasy; Fatigue; Faultfinding; Fear; Fear Of Accusation; Fear Of Condemnation; Fear Of Disapproval; Fear Of Failure; Fear Of Judgment. Chart numbers #476–486. Fruit: Destructive Identities Against God’s Image. Parent: Principality of Destructive Identities Against God.

What “listening” means here: not hearing a teaching in order to repent, but agreeing with the name as if it were you — rehearsing it, defending it, thinking from it, letting it interpret God, people, and the day. A familiar identity is not a passing mood. It is a worn name. Names bear fruit.

I. The Life Scripture Commands

Philippians 4:8–9 — “Finally, brothers, whatever is true, whatever is honorable, whatever is just, whatever is pure, whatever is lovely, whatever is commendable, if there is any excellence, if there is anything worthy of praise, think about these things. What you have learned and received and heard and seen in me — practice these things, and the God of peace will be with you.”

Philippians 4:12–13 — “I know how to be brought low, and I know how to abound. In any and every circumstance, I have learned the secret of facing plenty and hunger, abundance and need. I can do all things through him who strengthens me.”

Galatians 5:22–23 — “But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control; against such things there is no law.”

1 Corinthians 13:4–8a — “Love is patient and kind; love does not envy or boast; it is not arrogant or rude. It does not insist on its own way; it is not irritable or resentful; it does not rejoice at wrongdoing, but rejoices with the truth. Love bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things. Love never ends.”

Paul is not offering three hobby lists. He is describing one life: a mind that dwells on what is true and praiseworthy; a body that practices what was received; a soul that has learned contentment in Christ rather than in circumstance; the Spirit’s produce; and love that will not envy, keep score, or collapse under accusation. Day 48’s identities do not merely “feel unlike” that life. They denounce it. They teach the opposite gospel of the inner life: you are the wound, the comparison, the escape, the fault, the fear. Once that name is lord, Philippians, Galatians, and 1 Corinthians are treated as unsafe, naive, or for other people.

II. Why These Are “Fruits of Identities”

On the Topics 666 chart, fruits grow from roots. Day 48 sits in the familiar-identity orchard: the fruit is already a name against God’s image. Listening to the identity is how the fruit keeps seeding. You do not only envy; you become Envy. You do not only feel tired; you become Fatigue. You do not only startle; you become Fear of Judgment.

That is why listening counteracts Scripture. Scripture commands a new mind and a practiced walk (Phil 4:8–9). A familiar identity commands a loop: think from the name, practice the name, call the loop honesty. The God of peace will not share the house with a name that refuses to think on what is true.

III. Philippians 4 — The Mind and the Secret These Names Reverse

Philippians 4:8 is a gate for the mind. Eight tests: true, honorable, just, pure, lovely, commendable, excellent, praiseworthy. Then verse 9: practice. Then verses 12–13: learned contentment, strength in Christ in plenty and in want.

Listening to Day 48 walks the mind back out through that gate.

IV. Galatians 5 — Spirit-Fruit vs. Identity-Fruit

Paul sets the fruit of the Spirit against the works of the flesh (Gal 5:19–21): enmity, strife, jealousy, fits of anger, rivalries, dissensions, divisions, envy, and the rest. Those who practice such things will not inherit the kingdom. Day 48 is how some of that produce becomes a self. Jealousy and envy are not only acts; they become Familiar Identity Of Envy. The flesh is no longer something you did. It is someone you are.

The Spirit does not bear fruit on a throne occupied by a familiar name. Walking by the Spirit (Gal 5:16) includes refusing to gratify the flesh — including the flesh when it has learned to speak in the first person: I am Envy. I am Fatigue. I am Fear of Judgment.

V. 1 Corinthians 13 — Love Named, Then Named Against

Paul’s description of love is not poetry to frame a wedding. It is the operating system of the church. Day 48 listening writes a rival description.

To listen to Day 48 as identity is to denounce 1 Corinthians 13 in the only place it must live: the will. You can quote the chapter and still wear Envy. The chapter is then a witness against the name, not a decoration on it.

VI. Each Identity, Briefly Denounced by the Word

Envy (#476)

Love does not envy. The Spirit’s fruit includes kindness and goodness toward the one who has what you wanted. Philippians 4:12 learns plenty without theft of another’s portion. Listening to envy denounces all three: it calls another’s gift an injustice, God’s allotment a mistake, and love a fool.

Escapism (#477)

Practice requires remaining (Phil 4:9). Contentment requires inhabiting plenty and want (Phil 4:12). Faithfulness is staying. Escapism denounces incarnation: it will not be where God put the body. Loneliness is the root; the fruit is a name that never has to be known.

Fantasy (#478)

“Whatever is true.” Love rejoices with the truth. Fantasy from deception is a counterfeit Philippians 4:8 — a mind full of pictures that will not submit to what is so. It denounces the God who speaks in history, not in a private novel.

Fatigue (#479)

Bodies tire; that is not the identity. The name Fatigue says weakness is the self, so Philippians 4:13 is offensive. The Spirit’s joy and self-control look like demands. Listening to fatigue-as-self denounces strengthening in Christ and baptizes withdrawal as realism.

Faultfinding (#480)

Love is kind, not irritable, not resentful; it does not rejoice in wrongdoing but is not a scavenger of faults either. Pride finds fault to stay above. That denounces gentleness and the command to think on what is commendable. A church of faultfinders cannot bear Galatians fruit; it bears Galatians flesh: strife, dissensions, divisions.

Fear (#481) and the five fears (#482–486)

Perfect love casts out fear (1 John 4:18). The Spirit gives power, love, and self-control, not a spirit of fear (2 Tim 1:7). There is now no condemnation in Christ Jesus (Rom 8:1). The fear of the Lord is clean; fear of accusation, condemnation, disapproval, failure, and judgment as identities are not the fear of the Lord. They are insecurity and shame wearing theology. They denounce peace, they denounce “think on what is true” (the true verdict is Christ’s), they denounce love that believes and hopes, and they denounce “I can do all things through him” because they have already written the verdict: accused, condemned, disapproved, failed, judged.

Pastoral line: naming a spirit of fear in order to repent is obedience. Wearing Fear of Judgment as who you are is agreement. Day 48 teaching is for eviction, not for baptism of the name.

VII. Summary

The Bible’s life: a mind gated by what is true and praiseworthy; a practiced walk; contentment and strength in Christ in every circumstance; the fruit of the Spirit; love that does not envy, keeps no resentment, rejoices with the truth, and does not end.

Day 48’s fruits of identities: envy, escape, fantasy, fatigue, faultfinding, and fear in five costumes. Each is a name against God’s image. Listening — agreeing, meditating, living from the name — is how the fruit keeps reproducing.

How listening counteracts Scripture: it commandeers the same inner faculties Paul commands. Thought, practice, circumstance, and love are all reinterpreted from the identity. Philippians 4 is treated as denial. Galatians 5 fruit is treated as unsafe until the identity is soothed. 1 Corinthians 13 is quoted and disobeyed in the same hour, especially at “love does not envy.”

The denunciation: these names do not merely fail to resemble the Christian life. They preach against it. They say you are comparison, exit, invention, depletion, critique, and alarm — not a child who can think, practice, be content, bear the Spirit’s fruit, and love. Repentance is refusing the name and taking again the mind of Christ.
