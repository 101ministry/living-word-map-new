Scripture text: Berean Standard Bible (public domain; formerly called the Berean Study Bible). Every passage named below is quoted in full from the BSB. Where the BSB does not print a King James verse number, that number is omitted here as well.

A word on the English word hell. The Berean Standard Bible does not fold every original word into one English hell. In the Old Testament it usually says Sheol for the realm of the dead. In the New Testament it may say Hades (the realm of the dead), hell (Gehenna, the valley used as a picture of final judgment), or, in 2 Peter 2:4, hell for Tartarus (the pit where certain angels are reserved). Those places are not the same. This study keeps the Berean Standard wording and then states what the passage is actually describing.

The question in the title is what the Bible records, what it does not record, and what Repentance101 Ministry holds from the Lord beyond the page. The Bible does not specifically describe Jesus being tortured in hell. It also does not explain how, as a man on the earth, He knew the details He taught about hell. It does not record demons ignoring Him there, or the prophets honored as guests of the fire. It does record that He taught about hell with authority, that a righteous beggar is comforted while a rich man is tormented, that His soul was not abandoned to Hades, and that on the earth the demons knew Him and obeyed Him. After the resurrection He spent forty days with the disciples speaking of the kingdom. The Gospels were written later as eyewitness testimony. The ministry's stance is that Jesus did go to hell.

III. DID JESUS GO TO HELL AND WAS HE IGNORED BY THE DEMONS?

No passage describes demons ignoring Jesus in hell. The passages that speak of His death, burial, and descent speak of hell as the realm of the dead, of paradise, of preaching to spirits, and of triumph over rulers. None of that is neglect by demons. None of that is torture.

VIII. CONCLUSION

How did Jesus know about hell? The written Gospels do not explain the source of His detail. He was a man on the earth, led by the Spirit, set on His Father's will, yet that still does not account for the precision of His teaching. After He rose, He opened the Scriptures and spoke forty days of the kingdom. The four Gospels were written later as eyewitness testimony, many of them fifty to ninety years after the ascension, so it is more than plausible that what the risen Lord taught filled what the disciples later wrote.
