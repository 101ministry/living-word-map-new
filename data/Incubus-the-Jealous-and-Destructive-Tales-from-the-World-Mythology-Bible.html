Incubus - the Jealous & Destructive - Tales from the World, from Mythology, from the Bible

9/21/2026 — Berean Standard Bible, quoted in full

This study is the counterpart to the succubus sheet. That one named the lure that calls a man off holiness. This one names the possessive man who will do anything to keep a woman from walking in the ways God has designed: prayer, church, a clean conscience, a calling, a covenant that is not a cage, and the fear of the LORD rather than the fear of him.

Christ loves the church and gives Himself up for her, to make her holy. The incubus pattern does the opposite. He takes, isolates, monitors, threatens, and calls it love. In Topics 666 this sits with jealousy (011), being controlling (027), being domineering (028), being manipulative (121), cruel oppression (240), and Spirit Spouse material that treats a person as owned. The title word incubus is later folklore for a night spirit in male form. It is not a Berean Bible word. It is used here as a handle for jealous destruction across world, myth, and Scripture.

Writing is kept PG. Myths are folk warnings, not a second Bible. Some Bible narratives name force; they are quoted as written, without added detail. Scripture text: Berean Standard Bible (public domain; formerly called the Berean Study Bible). Every passage named below is quoted in full from the BSB.

I. TALES FROM THE WORLD

In first-world and second-world countries he rarely introduces himself as a jailer. He is framed as protector, provider, traditional husband, wounded romantic, or "the only one who really loves her." The fruit is the same: her world shrinks until God's design for her cannot be walked. He is jealous of her Lord, her church, her friends, her work, her children, and even her rest.

First-world countries

United States, Western Europe, Canada, Australia, Japan, South Korea, and similar.

  • Coercive control without a bruise the camera can see. He reads her messages, tracks her phone, decides who she may see, and punishes silence with rage or with a cold house. The law in several countries now names this as a crime. The spirit is older than the statute.
  • Love as a hostage. If you leave me I will ruin myself, ruin you, or ruin the children. Proverbs 6 already knew a jealous man who will not be bought off.
  • Headship as a costume. He quotes wives-submit and skips husbands-love, do-not-be-harsh, and fellow-heirs. Biblical headship in Ephesians 5 is Christ giving Himself. This man uses the verse as a leash.
  • Isolation from the gathering. Church is "full of hypocrites." Women's Bible study is "gossip." Prayer is allowed if he can hear it. Suppressing ministries and ministers (topics 154-155) often begins in a living room.
  • Image and reputation. In public he is charming. In private the air changes. Friends are shown a devoted couple. She is told no one will believe her.
  • After she leaves. Stalking, false reports, dragging out courts, posting her image to shame her. "If I cannot have you, no one will, and God will not either."
  • The night version. He does not have to kick a door. He can fill a house with demand, with accusation, and with a secret life that treats her body as property and her soul as a rival.

Second-world countries

Much of Eastern Europe, Russia and neighbors, large parts of Latin America, Turkey, Mexico, and similar middle-income regions.

  • Honor as ownership. Her obedience is the family's face. A walk toward God that he did not authorize is treated as shame. The threat may be the in-laws, the street, or a quiet accident.
  • Bride-price and papers as a bill of sale. Marriage arranged for money, visa, or clan. She is not asked whether she will fear the LORD. She is moved like livestock. Shechem wanted Dinah as a wife after he had already taken her. The sequence is the tell.
  • Religion as a lock. He may be louder in ritual than she is, yet forbid her a living faith, a female teacher, or a church that would hear her cry. Jezebel taught immorality in a church. This man forbids holiness in a home.
  • Work and money as a gate. She may not have an account, a passport, or a job. God's design includes stewardship. He calls her dependence faithfulness.
  • The forest and the city both. In some regions abduction-marriage is still named. In cities the same spirit uses apartments, nightclubs, and "protection." Either way she is kept off the road.

What both worlds share: he is jealous, and the jealousy is destructive. He does not want her holy. He wants her his. If holiness would make her brave, he will starve it. If a church would make her seen, he will mock it. If God would be her Husband in the prophetic sense, he will compete with God.

II. TALES FROM MYTHOLOGY

These are not Scripture. They are how many nations remembered a male figure who seizes, hides, binds, or visits in the night so that a woman cannot go home, cannot keep a vow, and cannot belong to anyone but him. Read them as folk memory of the same snare. Where old religions named spirits and idols, the New Testament says the nations sacrificed to demons, not to God.

1 Corinthians 10:19-21

19Am I suggesting, then, that food sacrificed to an idol is anything, or that an idol is anything? 20No, but the sacrifices of pagans are offered to demons, not to God. And I do not want you to be participants with demons. 21You cannot drink the cup of the Lord and the cup of demons too; you cannot partake in the table of the Lord and the table of demons too.

The tales below stay PG. The danger is captivity: a stolen cloak, a locked tower, a night claim, not a scene to repeat. Each named term has a short hear MP3. Japanese names are spoken in Japanese. English literary names are spoken in English. Tap or click hear; in the PDF the same word is a link to the clip.

Night visitors and spirit-husbands

  • Incubus hear (medieval European lore). The male counterpart of the succubus. A night spirit said to press upon a woman in sleep, leaving her exhausted and bound to a secret. PG meaning: a claim on her rest, her body, and her attention that holiness cannot share. This is the title's folk name. In many churches on several continents the same pattern is still named a spirit husband: a night visitor who forbids marriage, prayer, or a clean house.
  • Asmodeus hear in the book of Tobit (not in the 66-book Protestant canon, but widely told). A destroyer who will not let a woman belong to a husband. The folk point: jealous death standing in the doorway of a covenant.
  • Jinn hear marriages in parts of the Middle East and North Africa. Stories of a hidden husband who is not human, who is angry if she prays, weds, or is blessed. The warning is the same as Spirit Spouse: you do not owe a demon a wedding.

Stolen skin, stolen road

  • Selkie hear and swan-maiden hear tales (Celtic, Norse, and wide Eurasian variants). A man finds a woman's seal-skin or feather-cloak and hides it. Without it she cannot return to her own kind or her own sea. He calls it marriage. The tale calls it theft. God's design for her is the cloak he will not give back.
  • Bluebeard hear. A husband with a locked room and a rule that is not for her good. Curiosity is blamed. The locked room is his. She is not allowed to know the truth of the house.
  • Hades hear and Persephone hear (Greek). The underworld king takes a young woman from the field. The mother searches. The year splits. Whether later poets soften it, the old shape is abduction dressed as a wedding.

Gods and hunters who will not stop

  • Apollo hear and Daphne hear, and other chase-stories. A god pursues. She runs. She would rather become a tree than be caught. PG meaning: pursuit that will not hear no, even when the woman flees into another form.
  • Satyrs hear and similar woodland males. Folklore of appetite without covenant. They do not keep a home. They raid one.
  • Oni hear (Japanese おに), mountain bandits, and bride-stealing yōkai hear (Japanese ようかい). Processions interrupted. A woman carried off the road to a hidden place. The village tale is a map: do not let the mountain take her.

What the myths keep repeating

He is not first known as a brother in covenant. He takes. He hides the thing she needs in order to be free. He visits at night. He is jealous of every other claim, including heaven. The cost is her voice, her road, her vow, or her life. The dragon in Revelation 12 pursues the woman and then makes war on her children. Mythology painted smaller dragons. The Bible names the rage.

III. TALES FROM THE BIBLE

Fallen rule over the woman appears as soon as sin does. Then come men who take daughters, display queens, seize wives, and call force love. Then come the Lord's commands that break the pattern: love, honor, no harshness, fellow heirs. Then the dragon who hunts the woman because she bears the child and the testimony.

The fracture and the boast

Genesis 3:16

16To the woman He said: “I will sharply increase your pain in childbirth; in pain you will bring forth children. Your desire will be for your husband, and he will rule over you.”

This is spoken after the fall. It is not the design of Eden. The possessive man treats the wound as a warrant.

Genesis 4:19-24

And the sister of Tubal-cain was Naamah. 23Then Lamech said to his wives: “Adah and Zillah, hear my voice; wives of Lamech, listen to my speech. For I have slain a man for wounding me, a young man for striking me. 24If Cain is avenged sevenfold, then Lamech seventy-sevenfold.”

Later folklore read this as the incubus's old family: taking women, not asking, making a name. The BSB text is enough: they took as wives whomever they chose.

Shechem's "love" comes after force, and he keeps her in his house until her brothers take her out. Possessive desire is not the same as covenant.

2 Samuel 13:1-2, 11-17, 19-20

This is the incubus pattern without a Latin name: appetite called love, force, then hatred, then a door bolted so she cannot even have the false covering of marriage. She is left desolate. That is how a man keeps a woman from God's open design: shame, silence, and a locked door.

The dragon who pursues the woman

Revelation 12:13-17

13And when the dragon saw that he had been thrown to the earth, he pursued the woman who had given birth to the male child. 14But the woman was given two wings of a great eagle to fly from the presence of the serpent to her place in the wilderness, where she was nourished for a time, and times, and half a time. 15Then from his mouth the serpent spewed water like a river to overtake the woman and sweep her away in the torrent. 16But the earth helped the woman and opened its mouth to swallow up the river that the dragon had poured from his mouth. 17And the dragon was enraged at the woman and went to make war with the rest of her children, who keep the commandments of God and hold to the testimony of Jesus. And the dragon stood on the shore of the sea.

This is the cosmic incubus: rage at the woman because of the child and the testimony. He cannot keep her, so he tries to sweep her away, then he makes war on her children. A household tyrant is a small picture of that dragon. God's design still includes wings, wilderness provision, and a people who keep the commandments and the testimony of Jesus.

V. CLOSE

The world sells the possessive man as protector, provider, or wounded lover. Mythology painted him as incubus, skin-thief, locked-room husband, hunter-god, and spirit-husband. The Bible names Lamech's boast, sons of God who took, Shechem who took then asked, Amnon who forced then hated, Xerxes who would display a queen, David who sent to get a wife, Nabal who was harsh, husbands who cover the garment with violence, and the dragon who pursues the woman.

Christ's pattern is the opposite: He gives Himself up to make her holy. Husbands are told to love, not to be harsh, and to honor a fellow heir, or their prayers are hindered. Charm in the succubus study was a lure. Jealousy in this study is a chain. Both keep a soul from walking in the ways God has designed. The man who pleases God does not compete with God for a woman. He nourishes. He cherishes. He gets out of the way of her fear of the LORD.
