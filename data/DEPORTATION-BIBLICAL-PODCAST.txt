Welcome back. Today we are doing something deliberate and uncomfortable on purpose.
We are not starting with politics, headlines, or party lines.
We are starting with Scripture — read aloud, in full — on three questions that
must be held together, not torn apart:

  1. What does the Bible say about the law of the land, governing authority,
     and the expectation that people living under that authority obey its laws?

  2. What does the Bible say about the love of Christ and how we are to love
     our neighbor?

  3. What does the Bible say about deportation, expulsion, exile, removal from
     the land, and the breaking of covenants with nations — and why?

Only after we have let the text speak do we ask the modern question:
Can we come to terms with deportation biblically today?

================================================================================

[HOST]

Question before we read: Does the Bible treat civil order as a side issue,
or as something God himself instituted?

[CO-HOST]

It treats it as instituted. Not perfect. Not ultimate. But real — and accountable

GENESIS 9:5–6 — Bloodguilt and the Image of God
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

BLB PASSAGE: https://www.blueletterbible.org/esv/gen/9/5-6/
BLB COMMENTARIES:
  Matthew Henry — https://www.blueletterbible.org/Comm/mhc/Gen/Gen_009.cfm
  MacArthur (Genesis Intro) — https://www.blueletterbible.org/comm/macarthur_john/bible-introductions/genesis-intro.cfm
  Chuck Smith C2000 — https://www.blueletterbible.org/Comm/smith_chuck/c2000_Gen/Gen_009.cfm

[HOST reads — ESV:]

And for your lifeblood I will require a reckoning: from every beast I will
require it and from man. From his fellow man I will require a reckoning for
the life of man.

"Whoever sheds the blood of man,
    by man shall his blood be shed,
for God made man in his own image."

[COMMENTARY — Matthew Henry, via BLB (Genesis 9:6 — selective excerpt):]

"The magistrate must punish murderers: Whoso sheddeth man's blood, by man
shall his blood be shed. Before the flood, God took the punishment of murder
into his own hands; but now he committed this judgment to men, to masters of
families at first, and afterwards to the heads of countries. Wilful murder
ought always to be punished with death. The reason is because man is made in
God's image — a sinner against God therefore, as well as against man, and
must be put to death as an outlaw and a rebel against God and his kingdom."

[COMMENTARY — John MacArthur, via BLB (Genesis Introduction):]

MacArthur frames Genesis as the book of beginnings — including the beginning
of human government under God after the Flood. The Noahic covenant establishes
that life is sacred because man bears God's image, and that human authority
exists to protect that image. This is pre-Mosaic, pre-Israel — civil order
as a creation ordinance, not a Jewish peculiarity.

[CO-HOST — Q&A]

Does this mean every government is good?

[HOST]

No — it means every government is accountable. Genesis 9 gives the foundation;
later texts show what happens when governments fail. Chuck Smith's C2000 study
on Genesis 9 on BLB walks the same ground: God institutes order before Sinai.

EXODUS 21–23 — The Book of the Covenant (Civil and Judicial Law)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[HOST]

Exodus 21 through 23 is the detailed case law that implements the Ten
Commandments in daily life — slavery regulations, restitution for injury,
property law, judicial integrity, Sabbath and festival observance, and
commands against oppression. We read it as one body of law because that is
how Scripture presents it.

[HOST reads — opening of the code, Exodus 21:1–27:]

"Now these are the rules that you shall set before them. When you buy a Hebrew
servant, he shall serve six years, and in the seventh he shall go out free,
for nothing. If he comes in single, he shall go out single; if he comes in
married, then his wife shall go out with him. If his master gives him a wife
and she bears him sons or daughters, the wife and her children shall be her
master's, and he shall go out alone. But if the servant plainly says, 'I love
my master, my wife, and my children; I will not go out free,' then his master
shall bring him to God, and he shall bring him to the door or the doorpost.
And his master shall bore his ear through with an awl, and he shall be his
servant for life.

"When a man sells his daughter as a servant, she shall not go out as the male
servants do. If she does not please her master, who has designated her for
himself, then he shall let her be redeemed. He shall have no right to sell her
to a foreign people, since he has broken faith with her. If he designates her
for his son, he shall deal with her as with a daughter. If he takes another
wife to himself, he shall not diminish her food, her clothing, or her marital
rights. And if he does not do these three things for her, she shall go out
for nothing, without payment of money.

"Whoever strikes a man so that he dies shall be put to death. But if he did
not lie in wait for him, but God let him fall into his hand, then I will
appoint for you a place to which he may flee. But if a man willfully attacks
another to kill him by cunning, you shall take him from my altar, that he may
die.

"Whoever strikes his father or his mother shall be put to death.

"Whoever steals a man and sells him, and anyone found in possession of him,
shall be put to death.

"Whoever curses his father or his mother shall be put to death.

"When men quarrel and one strikes the other with a stone or with his fist and
the man does not die but takes to his bed, then if the man rises again and
walks outdoors with his staff, he who struck him shall be cleared; only he
shall pay for the loss of his time, and shall have him thoroughly healed.

"When a man strikes his servant, male or female, with a rod and the servant
dies under his hand, he shall be avenged. But if the servant survives a day or
two, he is not to be avenged, for the servant is his money.

"When men strive together and hit a pregnant woman, so that her children come
out, but there is no harm, the one who hit her shall surely be fined, as the
woman's husband shall impose on him, and he shall pay as the judges determine.
But if there is harm, then you shall pay life for life, eye for eye, tooth
for tooth, hand for hand, foot for foot, burn for burn, wound for wound,
stripe for stripe.

"When a man strikes the eye of his servant, male or female, and destroys it,
he shall let the servant go free because of his eye. If he knocks out the tooth
of his servant, male or female, he shall let the servant go free because of
his tooth."

[HOST — continues with representative sections:]

Exodus 21:28–36 — liability for oxen that gore.
Exodus 22:1–15 — restitution for theft and property damage.
Exodus 22:16–31 — social and moral offenses, including sorcery, bestiality,
  idolatry, oppression of sojourners, widows, and orphans.
Exodus 23:1–9 — perverted justice forbidden; partiality forbidden; the poor
  not denied justice; bribes forbidden; the sojourner not oppressed.
Exodus 23:10–19 — Sabbath years, Sabbath days, feasts.
Exodus 23:20–33 — angelic guidance; warning against covenanting with
  Canaanite nations; gradual dispossession of the land's inhabitants.

[HOST reads — Exodus 23:1–9:]

"You shall not spread a false report. You shall not join hands with a wicked
man to be a malicious witness. You shall not fall in with the many to do evil,
nor shall you bear witness in a lawsuit, siding with the many, so as to
pervert justice, nor shall you be partial to a poor man in his lawsuit.

"If you meet your enemy's ox or his donkey going astray, you shall bring it
back to him. If you see the donkey of one who hates you lying down under its
burden, you shall refrain from leaving him with it; you shall rescue it with
him.

"You shall not pervert the justice due to your poor in his lawsuit. Keep far
from a false charge, and do not kill the innocent and righteous, for I will
not acquit the wicked. And you shall take no bribe, for a bribe blinds the
clear-sighted and subverts the cause of those who are in the right.

"You shall not oppress a sojourner. You know the heart of a sojourner, for
you were sojourners in the land of Egypt."

[CO-HOST]

Q&A: Does this law code only bind Israel, or does it reveal God's character
for all nations?

[HOST]

Israel receives it as covenant law. But the repeated concern for impartial
justice, protection of the vulnerable, and prohibition of bribery and false
witness is presented as what righteous governance looks like wherever God's
people live.

[COMMENTARY — Matthew Henry, via BLB (Matthew 22:15-22 — selective excerpt):]

"Render therefore to Caesar the things that are Caesar's: When we render to
Caesar the things that are Caesar's, we must remember withal to render to God

nature — his kingdom is not of this world."

[COMMENTARY — Chuck Smith, via BLB (C2000, Romans 13):]

"As Christians, what should be our attitude towards government? Paul declares,
'Let every soul be subject unto the higher powers. For there is no power but
of God: and the powers that be are ordained of God.'

The Bible does not allow for civil disobedience — for we are commanded by the
scriptures to be in obedience to those governing bodies that are over us. Paul
wrote this at the time in which Nero was ruling in Rome. We oftentimes say,
'Well, we should be in obedience as long as we agree with what is being
legislated.' I do feel that there are rare occasions where the law of God does
supercede the law of man, and on those occasions I must be obedient to God.

When they were required to declare that Caesar was lord or be executed, they
chose death by martyrdom. When Peter was ordered not to speak anymore in the
name of Jesus, he said, 'Whether it is right to obey God or man, you judge.'

Why does God allow evil men to reign? Basically, because men want evil men to
reign over them, and in order that they might be brought to judgment, God will
allow those evil rulers. But I am told here as a child of God to be subject
unto those higher powers because they have been established there by God."

[COMMENTARY — Matthew Henry, via BLB (Romans 13:1-2 — selective excerpt):]

"Let every soul be subject — every person, not excluding the clergy. It
intimates that our subjection must be free and voluntary, sincere and hearty.
There is no power but of God — civil power is derived from him as its original.
The usurpation of power and the abuse of power are not of God; but the power
itself is. We must be subject, not only for wrath, but for conscience' sake —
not so much from the fear of punishment, as from the love of virtue."

[COMMENTARY — John MacArthur, via BLB (Romans Introduction):]

"Some have ignored Paul's teaching on the believer's obedience to human
government (13:1-7) in the name of Christian activism, while others have used
it to defend slavish obedience to totalitarian regimes." MacArthur's Romans
introduction warns against both errors — the text is real, but not absolute.

[CO-HOST — Q&A]

[HOST]

Chuck Smith and MacArthur both draw the line at Acts 4:19. Smith: rare
occasions when God's law supersedes man's. Henry: subjection is for
conscience's sake, not terror alone. Paul wrote under Nero — the text assumes
government punishing evil, not requiring evil worship.

PART THREE — DEPORTATION, EXPULSION, EXILE, AND COVENANT SEPARATION
================================================================================

[HOST]

Part Three is where people expect a simple yes or no. Scripture does not give
one. It gives patterns — expulsion for sin, exile for covenant-breaking,
dispossession of nations for abomination, separation for idolatry — alongside
commands to love the sojourner and welcome foreigners who join the covenant.
We read all of it and let the commentators help us see the reasons given.

GENESIS 3:22–24 — Adam and Eve Expelled from Eden
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

BLB PASSAGE: https://www.blueletterbible.org/esv/gen/3/22-24/
BLB COMMENTARIES:
  Matthew Henry — https://www.blueletterbible.org/Comm/mhc/Gen/Gen_003.cfm
  Chuck Smith C2000 — https://www.blueletterbible.org/Comm/smith_chuck/c2000_Gen/Gen_003.cfm

[HOST reads — ESV:]

Then the LORD God said, "Behold, the man has become like one of us in knowing
good and evil. Now, lest he reach out his hand and take also of the tree of life
and eat, and live forever—" therefore the LORD God sent him out from the garden
of Eden to work the ground from which he was taken. He drove out the man, and at
the east of the garden of Eden he placed the cherubim and a flaming sword that
turned every way to guard the way to the tree of life.

REASON GIVEN: Rebellion against God's command; death entered; access to eternal
life in Eden closed until redemption.

[COMMENTARY — Matthew Henry, via BLB (Genesis 3:22-24 — selective excerpt):]

"God turned him out, and kept him out. He sent him forth from the garden to
the common — twice mentioned: he sent him forth, and then he drove him out.
Adam liked the place too well to be willing to part with it, and therefore
God drove him out, whether he would or no. This signified the exclusion from
that communion with God which was the bliss and glory of paradise.

He kept him out: cherubim with a flaming sword guarded the way to the tree of
life. Adam and his descendants were prevented from eating the fruit of life
— by God's mercy — yet could still come there to meet God. This is the last
historical mention of the garden of Eden in the Bible."

[CO-HOST — Q&A]

Is this "deportation"?

[HOST]

Henry calls it exclusion and expulsion — place, covenant, violation, removal.
The pattern begins here.

REASON GIVEN: Breaking faith with the God of Israel; covenant violation after

Henry does not pretend Ezra 10 is easy. The reason is covenant faithfulness —

LEVITICUS 19:33–34 — Love the Sojourner (Repeated — Both Pillars)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

BLB PASSAGE: https://www.blueletterbible.org/esv/lev/19/33-34/
BLB COMMENTARIES:
  Matthew Henry — https://www.blueletterbible.org/Comm/mhc/Lev/Lev_019.cfm
  Chuck Smith C2000 — https://www.blueletterbible.org/Comm/smith_chuck/c2000_Lev/Lev_019.cfm

[HOST reads — ESV:]

"When a stranger sojourns with you in your land, you shall not do him wrong.
You shall treat the stranger who sojourns with you as the native among you, and
you shall love him as yourself, for you were strangers in the land of Egypt: I

[COMMENTARY — Matthew Henry, via BLB (Leviticus 19:33-34 — selective excerpt):]

"Thou shalt not vex a stranger, but love him as thyself, and as one of thy own
people. Both the law of God and his providence had vastly dignified Israel above
any other people, yet they must not therefore trample upon all mankind but
those of their own nation.

It is supposed that this stranger was not an idolater, but a worshipper of the
God of Israel — at least a proselyte of the gate. They must not vex him, nor
oppress, nor over-reach him — they must reckon it as great a sin to cheat a
stranger as to cheat an Israelite. Love him as thyself; for you were strangers
in the land of Egypt."

[HOST — CLOSING SYNTHESIS]

We have read three bodies of Scripture — authority, love, exile — with help
from MacArthur, Chuck Smith, and Matthew Henry on Blue Letter Bible.

  1. GOVERNING AUTHORITY IS REAL (Genesis 9; Romans 13; 1 Peter 2).
     Chuck Smith: Paul wrote under Nero; obedience is the norm, Acts 4:19 the
     exception. MacArthur warns against both activism and slavish totalitarianism.

  2. LOVE OF NEIGHBOR IS NON-NEGOTIABLE (Leviticus 19; Matthew 22; 1 John 4).
     Henry: the sojourner who joins Israel's God is to be loved as yourself —
     cheating a stranger equals cheating an Israelite.

  3. REMOVAL FROM LAND HAS SPECIFIC COVENANT REASONS (Genesis 3; Deuteronomy 28;
     Exodus 23; Ezra 10). Henry consistently ties expulsion to sin, idolatry,
     or refused covenant — not ethnicity alone.

  4. WELCOMING FAITHFUL FOREIGNERS IS ALSO BIBLICAL (Ruth; Isaiah 56). Ezra's
     crisis was wives who refused the covenant — not foreigners who joined it.

[CO-HOST — FINAL Q&A]

So is deportation a biblical issue we can come to terms with today?

[HOST]

Scripture knows expulsion, exile, and separation — always with reasons given,
never as a substitute for love. You cannot strip Exodus 23 or Ezra 10 of
covenant context and paste them onto modern policy.

MacArthur, Smith, and Henry all resist that shortcut. Smith on Romans 13 will
not let you ignore Acts 4. Henry on Leviticus 19 will not let you ignore the
sojourner. Henry on Ezra 10 will not let you ignore Ruth.
